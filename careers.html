03 / PURPOSE & IMPACT · CAREER BY CAREER

What does each career make possible?

Each profession uses mathematical tools for a different human need. Here is what the work is for and how it can improve everyday life.

01DATA & EVIDENCE

Statistician

Mathematics used: Probability, statistics, regression, and statistical inference.

Purpose and impact

Statisticians design surveys and studies, then develop methods for interpreting the information they collect. Their work helps people distinguish real patterns from random variation. Governments use reliable statistics to understand social and economic conditions, while health researchers use statistical evidence to assess treatments. Better measurement helps institutions make decisions based on evidence rather than guesswork. Statistics Canada describes how mathematical statisticians develop methods for official statistics ↗

02RISK & SECURITY

Actuary

Mathematics used: Probability, statistics, financial mathematics, and risk theory.

Purpose and impact

Actuaries estimate how likely future events are and what their financial consequences could be. They use those estimates to help set insurance premiums, plan pension benefits, and keep long-term funds sustainable. This work affects practical choices such as the cost of protecting a home or car and the funds available for retirement. By helping organizations prepare for uncertainty, actuaries can make financial systems more stable for the people who depend on them. WorkBC’s occupational profile ↗

03DECISIONS & RESOURCES

Operations research analyst

Mathematics used: Optimization, linear algebra, probability, statistics, and algorithms.

Purpose and impact

Operations researchers turn complicated decisions into models that can be compared. An analyst might find how to schedule buses, route deliveries, assign aircraft, or distribute hospital resources while working within limits such as time, budget, and staff. The purpose is to help choose a strong option among competing goals. Better schedules and allocations can reduce waste, shorten delays, lower costs, and help essential services reach more people. WorkBC includes operations research analysts in its mathematics career profile ↗

04TRANSPORT & ACCESS

Transportation engineer

Mathematics used: Calculus, statistics, optimization, modeling, and geometry.

Purpose and impact

Transportation engineers model how people and goods move. They estimate travel demand, analyze traffic flows, improve intersections, design transit networks, and evaluate infrastructure plans. Their work can reduce congestion and travel times, improve road safety, and make transit more accessible. Reliable transportation connects people to schools, jobs, healthcare, businesses, and one another, so improvements can benefit whole communities.

05FINANCE & UNCERTAINTY

Quantitative analyst

Mathematics used: Statistics, probability, calculus, linear algebra, and stochastic processes.

Purpose and impact

Quantitative analysts use data and mathematical models to study financial markets, compare investment strategies, and estimate risk. Their models help financial organizations make decisions when future prices and outcomes are uncertain. This work can inform how investments are managed and how risk is measured. Testing models against data is important because a mathematical prediction is useful only when its limits and performance are understood.

06PRIVACY & COMMUNICATION

Cryptographer

Mathematics used: Number theory, abstract algebra, probability, and discrete mathematics.

Purpose and impact

Cryptographers develop and analyze methods that encode information so it can be read only by the intended people or systems. Digital banking, healthcare records, government services, online shopping, and private communication all rely on protecting sensitive information. Cryptography helps people communicate and make transactions with less risk of their data being exposed, supporting trust in the digital systems used in everyday life.

07PUBLIC HEALTH

Epidemiologist

Mathematics used: Statistics, probability, and mathematical modeling.

Purpose and impact

Epidemiologists study how diseases spread through populations and how patterns differ across places or groups. Statistical analysis and models help them estimate transmission and consider how interventions may change its course. Their findings inform public-health planning, from deciding where to focus prevention efforts to understanding how an outbreak is changing. This work can help communities reduce illness and prepare their health services.

08MEDICAL RESEARCH

Biostatistician

Mathematics used: Statistics, probability, and experimental design.

Purpose and impact

Biostatisticians help plan medical and biological studies so that the data can answer the research question, then analyze the results. Their work helps determine whether a treatment appears effective and whether an observed difference may be meaningful or due to chance. Careful study design and analysis make medical evidence more dependable, helping researchers and clinicians make better-informed choices about health and treatment.

09CLIMATE & ENVIRONMENT

Climate scientist / modeler

Mathematics used: Differential equations, statistics, and numerical modeling.

Purpose and impact

Climate modelers use equations and large datasets to represent interactions among the atmosphere, oceans, land, and ice. Models let researchers explore possible future conditions that cannot be observed directly and examine how outcomes change under different assumptions. This evidence helps communities and decision-makers understand environmental change and prepare plans for possible risks. Mathematical models turn complex physical processes into scenarios people can use to consider the future.

10FLIGHT & EXPLORATION

Aerospace engineer

Mathematics used: Calculus, differential equations, numerical methods, and geometry.

Purpose and impact

Aerospace engineers use mathematical models to design aircraft and spacecraft and predict how they will move and respond to physical forces. Calculations and simulations help engineers test designs and understand performance before building or operating a vehicle. Their work supports safer and more capable air travel, the movement of people and goods, and spacecraft that allow scientific instruments to study places far beyond Earth. 04 / FINAL REFLECTIONS

What stayed with me

How I grew as a researcher

I learned to look for evidence about both what a career does and why it matters. Comparing sources helped me see that they answer different questions: WorkBC describes occupations and related job titles, Statistics Canada shows how statistical methods support public information, and NASA gives examples of modeling in science and exploration. I also learned to connect broad math topics—such as probability or optimization—to specific work and real-world outcomes.

What surprised me

I was surprised by how many careers use mathematics outside of traditional math jobs. The same broad toolkit can help protect online information, plan transportation, evaluate medical treatments, model climate, or study the universe. I also learned that mathematics is not one skill: different areas of math answer very different kinds of questions.

Careers I would like to explore further

01

Operations Research Analyst

I would like to explore operations research in more detail because it combines mathematics with real-world decision-making. I am particularly interested in optimization: finding the best solution when there are constraints and competing objectives.

Transportation is one especially interesting application. I would like to investigate how mathematical models can determine transit routes, schedules, infrastructure investments, and other transportation decisions.

02

Quantitative Analyst

I would also like to explore quantitative analysis because it combines mathematics, statistics, and computer science to solve complex problems in finance. Quantitative analysts use mathematical models to analyze financial markets, evaluate risk, and make predictions about how different investments or strategies might perform.

I am particularly interested in how probability, statistics, and optimization can be used to make decisions when there is uncertainty. I would like to learn how mathematical models can turn large amounts of financial data into useful information and how these models can be tested to determine whether they actually provide an advantage.

One final thought

Mathematics can be both abstract and practical. Sometimes it creates new ideas; sometimes it helps people make a decision with limited time, money, or information. That range is what makes these careers interesting to me.
