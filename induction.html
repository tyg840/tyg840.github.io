The AM-GM Inequality

For nonnegative numbers, the arithmetic mean is always at least as large as the geometric mean. This proof uses Cauchy induction.

Statement

For a positive integer \(n\) and nonnegative real numbers \(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n\),

\[ \frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n}\geq\sqrt[n]{x_1x_2\cdots x_n}. \]

Base cases

For \(n=1\), both sides equal \(x_1\). For \(n=2\), the square \((\sqrt{x_1}-\sqrt{x_2})^2\) is nonnegative. Therefore,

\[ x_1+x_2\geq2\sqrt{x_1x_2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\geq\sqrt{x_1x_2}. \]

Doubling step

Assume AM-GM holds for \(k\) nonnegative numbers. Apply it to each half of a list of \(2k\) numbers, and define

\[ A=\sqrt[k]{x_1x_2\cdots x_k},\qquad B=\sqrt[k]{x_{k+1}x_{k+2}\cdots x_{2k}}. \]

The two applications give

\[ \frac{x_1+\cdots+x_{2k}}{2k}\geq\frac{A+B}{2}. \]

The base case \(n=2\), applied to \(A\) and \(B\), gives \(\frac{A+B}{2}\geq\sqrt{AB}\). Thus

\[ \frac{x_1+\cdots+x_{2k}}{2k}\geq\sqrt{AB}=\sqrt[2k]{x_1x_2\cdots x_{2k}}. \]

So if the result holds for \(k\), it also holds for \(2k\).

Step-down

Assume AM-GM holds for \(l\geq2\), and choose

\[ x_l=\frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_{l-1}}{l-1}. \]

The average of all \(l\) numbers is then \(x_l\). By the assumption,

\[ x_l\geq\sqrt[l]{x_1x_2\cdots x_{l-1}x_l}. \]

If \(x_l=0\), all of \(x_1,\dots,x_{l-1}\) are zero, so the inequality for those numbers holds. If \(x_l>0\), raising both sides to the \(l\)-th power and dividing by \(x_l\) gives

\[ x_l^{l-1}\geq x_1x_2\cdots x_{l-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{x_1+\cdots+x_{l-1}}{l-1}\geq\sqrt[l-1]{x_1x_2\cdots x_{l-1}}. \]

So if the result holds for \(l\), it also holds for \(l-1\).

Conclusion

Starting from \(n=1\) and \(n=2\), doubling proves the result for every power of two. For any positive integer \(n\), choose a power of two at least as large as \(n\), then repeatedly use the step-down result until reaching \(n\). Therefore AM-GM holds for every positive integer by Cauchy induction.
