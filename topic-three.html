02 / PURPOSE & IMPACT

Math helps people move forward.

Across these fields, the shared purpose is to make complicated systems easier to understand, improve decisions, and solve problems that affect everyday life.

↗

Knowledge & discovery

Mathematicians expand human knowledge by finding new relationships and structures. Applied mathematicians use models to explore what might happen when a system changes. Those tools support science, engineering, computing, and space exploration. NASA’s mathematician career spotlight ↗

⌁

Evidence for decisions

Statisticians and data scientists help distinguish meaningful patterns from random variation. Their methods inform medical research, public programs, and decisions made by governments and organizations. Statistics Canada explains how mathematical statisticians develop methods used to produce reliable official information. Statistics Canada: mathematical statistics careers ↗

◇

Planning for uncertainty

Actuaries estimate the likelihood and cost of future events, helping insurance and pension systems plan for risk. Operations researchers help choose among competing options, such as how to schedule buses, route deliveries, assign aircraft, or allocate hospital resources. WorkBC: mathematicians, statisticians and actuaries ↗

⇄

Health, safety & connection

Transportation engineers can help reduce congestion and improve safety and access. Cryptographers protect sensitive information used in banking, healthcare, government, and communication. Epidemiologists and biostatisticians help assess disease spread and whether treatments work.

◎

Understanding a changing planet

Climate scientists use equations and large datasets to represent interactions among the atmosphere, oceans, land, and ice. Models let researchers explore possible future conditions that cannot be observed directly. NASA’s model analyst career page describes modeling as a way to make complex data useful for decisions such as preparing for hurricanes. NASA: Mathematics model analyst ↗

03 / FINAL REFLECTIONS

What stayed with me

How I grew as a researcher

I learned to look for evidence about both what a career does and why it matters. Comparing sources helped me see that they answer different questions: WorkBC describes occupations and related job titles, Statistics Canada shows how statistical methods support public information, and NASA gives examples of modeling in science and exploration. I also learned to connect broad math topics—such as probability or optimization—to specific work and real-world outcomes.

What surprised me

I was surprised by how many careers use mathematics outside of traditional math jobs. The same broad toolkit can help protect online information, plan transportation, evaluate medical treatments, model climate, or study the universe. I also learned that mathematics is not one skill: different areas of math answer very different kinds of questions.

Careers I would like to explore further

01

Operations Research Analyst

I would like to explore operations research in more detail because it combines mathematics with real-world decision-making. I am particularly interested in optimization: finding the best solution when there are constraints and competing objectives.

Transportation is one especially interesting application. I would like to investigate how mathematical models can determine transit routes, schedules, infrastructure investments, and other transportation decisions.

02

Quantitative Analyst

I would also like to explore quantitative analysis because it combines mathematics, statistics, and computer science to solve complex problems in finance. Quantitative analysts use mathematical models to analyze financial markets, evaluate risk, and make predictions about how different investments or strategies might perform.

I am particularly interested in how probability, statistics, and optimization can be used to make decisions when there is uncertainty. I would like to learn how mathematical models can turn large amounts of financial data into useful information and how these models can be tested to determine whether they actually provide an advantage.

One final thought

Mathematics can be both abstract and practical. Sometimes it creates new ideas; sometimes it helps people make a decision with limited time, money, or information. That range is what makes these careers interesting to me.
